# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. This project exists because serious illness is disorganising and frightening,
and some of the people who show up here will be dealing with exactly that. Assume that.

## What is expected

- Treat disagreement as a technical matter, not a personal one.
- Accept that people arrive with different levels of experience, different languages, and
  different amounts of energy to spare.
- Take a correction gracefully, and give one kindly.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, insults, and personal attacks. Discriminatory language of any kind — including about
health status or disability. Publishing anyone's private information. Sustained disruption of
discussion. Sexual attention or advances.

Anything a reasonable person would recognise as making a space unwelcoming belongs on this list
whether or not it is named above.

## Medical privacy

Specific to this project, and taken seriously: **do not post real patient data anywhere in this
repository** — not in an issue, not in a pull request, not in a screenshot, not in a test fixture.
Redact before you attach. If you post something by accident, say so quickly; a maintainer can
delete it, though anything public should be assumed to have been seen.

Nothing here is medical advice, and nobody should offer any.

## Scope

This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, and anywhere someone is representing the
project.

## Reporting

Report a problem to the maintainer, [@arielland](https://github.com/arielland), by direct message
or by opening a private security advisory if the matter is sensitive. Reports are handled
privately. This is a single-maintainer project, so if your report concerns the maintainer,
GitHub's own [abuse reporting](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse) is the route that does not
run through them.

## Enforcement

The maintainer may edit or delete content, and may block a contributor, with a warning first where
a warning is likely to help and without one where it is not.
